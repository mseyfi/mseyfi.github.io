Video understanding

Video encoders, Video-LLMs, action recognition, key moments, retrieval, fingerprinting and Video-RAG.

201

Video as data

  • Frames, fps, resolution; codecs and GOPs (I, P, B frames)
  • Decoding cost is often the hidden bottleneck; GPU decode (NVDEC)
  • Sampling: uniform, keyframes, scene-based, adaptive
  • Tokens per second of video: the budget problem

202

Video encoders: from 3D CNNs to video transformers

  • I3D (inflated 2D filters) and SlowFast (two speeds); axial and factorized attention
  • ViViT and TimeSformer: divided space–time attention
  • VideoMAE: masked video pretraining with very high masking ratios
  • Video–text encoders such as InternVideo2
Ready
203

Action recognition

  • Clip-level vs video-level classification; datasets such as Kinetics
  • Temporal action localization: start, end and class
  • Zero-shot action recognition with video–text models
  • Sports actions are short, fast and fine-grained: what makes them hard
Ready
204

Video object segmentation and tracking: from VOS to SAM 2

  • Semi-supervised video object segmentation: mask in frame 1, follow the object
  • Memory-based matching across frames (XMem)
  • SAM 2: promptable segmentation with a streaming memory; points, boxes and masks as prompts
  • Referring segmentation from a sentence; metrics J and F

205

Video-LLMs

  • Frame sampling + per-frame encoder + temporal merging + LLM
  • Time encoding: frame index, timestamps as text, absolute time in M-RoPE
  • Video instruction data; joint image and video training
  • Case studies: Video-LLaVA, LLaVA-Video, Qwen2.5-VL video

206

Long video

  • Token compression across frames: merge similar tokens over time
  • Memory banks and streaming video LLMs
  • Agentic search: the model chooses which segments to look at
  • Captions as a compressed memory of the video

207

Temporal grounding and key-moment detection

  • Moment retrieval: given text, return the start and end time
  • Highlight detection: score each clip by importance
  • DETR-style detectors (Moment-DETR) vs LLMs that write timestamps (TimeChat)
  • Metrics: R1@IoU and mAP; label noise in “key moments”
Ready
208

Text-to-video retrieval

  • Frame embeddings + pooling (CLIP4Clip): mean, sequential, attention
  • Clip-level vs segment-level indexes; choosing the segment length
  • Video–text encoders (ViCLIP, InternVideo2)
  • Scaling to millions of segments; re-ranking with a VLM
Ready
209

Video fingerprinting and near-duplicate detection

  • Goal: the same content after crop, resize, re-encode, logo or speed change; not the same meaning
  • Perceptual hashes vs learned per-frame descriptors (SSCD-style)
  • Temporal alignment of matches (similarity matrices, ViSiL-style)
  • Evaluation: precision–recall, micro-AP, robustness tests
Ready

Video question answering and Video-RAG

  • Captions, ASR transcripts and OCR as searchable memory
  • Indexes at several levels: shot, scene, whole game
  • When to use Video-RAG vs a long-context video model
