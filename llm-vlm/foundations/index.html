Math you need, part 1: vectors, dot products, matrices and softmax

  • Vectors as lists of features; the dot product as a similarity score
  • Matrix multiply = many dot products at once; the shape rule (B, T, d)
  • Softmax turns scores into probabilities; temperature makes it sharp or flat
  • Numerical stability: subtract the max before exp

Math you need, part 2: probability, cross-entropy, KL divergence and perplexity

  • Maximum likelihood = minimize the negative log-likelihood
  • Cross-entropy between data and model; KL as the extra cost of a wrong model
  • Perplexity = exp(average loss)
  • Nats vs bits; a loss of 2.0 nats means perplexity ≈ 7.4

Language modeling: next-token prediction, from n-grams to neural models

  • Chain rule: the probability of a text is a product of next-token probabilities
  • Autoregressive generation: predict, append, repeat
  • n-gram models as the simple baseline, and why they fail (sparsity, short memory)
  • Teacher forcing in training vs free running at inference

  • RNN hidden state as a running summary; vanishing gradients
  • LSTM gates as memory control
  • Seq2seq encoder–decoder and its one-vector bottleneck
  • Bahdanau attention: the decoder looks back at all encoder states. Transformers keep this idea.

  • Characters vs words vs subwords: vocabulary size vs sequence length
  • Byte-level BPE step by step: count pairs, merge the most common, repeat
  • WordPiece and Unigram (SentencePiece): how they differ

Special tokens, chat templates and tokenizer pitfalls

  • BOS, EOS, PAD, UNK; BERT’s CLS, SEP, MASK: what each one is for

  • Placeholder tokens for images, audio and video in multimodal models
  • Pitfalls: numbers, spaces, glitch tokens, and multilingual cost (Farsi or Arabic often need more tokens per word than English)
