  • DINO: a student matches a teacher on different views of the same image
  • DINOv2 and DINOv3: strong dense features for detection, segmentation, tracking
  • Global vs dense features: which to use for VLMs and for video

CLIP: learning images and text together

  • Two encoders, one shared space
  • Symmetric InfoNCE over a batch of image–text pairs; learned temperature
  • Zero-shot classification with prompts (“a photo of a {label}”)
  • Limits: counting, spatial relations, small text, web-data bias

SigLIP and SigLIP 2

  • Sigmoid loss: each image–text pair is its own yes/no question
  • No softmax over the whole batch: easier scaling and smaller batches
  • SigLIP 2: better localization and dense features, flexible-resolution variants, many languages
  • Why many open VLMs use a SigLIP-family encoder

First-generation vision-language models

The ideas before LLM-based VLMs: two-stream and one-stream models, ALBEF and BLIP, grounded detectors, early video–language models.

172

Two-stream and single-stream VLMs: ViLBERT, LXMERT, UNITER

  • Image regions from an object detector become visual tokens
  • Two streams with co-attention (ViLBERT, LXMERT) vs one joint stream (UNITER)
  • Pretraining tasks: masked words with the image, masked regions, image–text matching
  • Why they faded: slow detectors, small paired data, no generation
Ready
173

ALBEF and BLIP: contrastive, matching and captioning losses together

  • Image–text contrastive loss (ITC) aligns the two encoders
  • Image–text matching (ITM) with hard negatives checks fine details
  • Captioning loss (LM) lets the model write text
  • BLIP’s CapFilt: generate new captions and filter noisy web captions
Ready
174

Grounded pretraining: GLIP and YOLO-World

  • Detection rewritten as phrase grounding: match regions to words
  • Region–word alignment scores replace a fixed class head
  • YOLO-World: real-time open-vocabulary detection; encode the vocabulary once, then detect
  • Where these beat VLMs: speed, boxes, small objects
Ready
175

Early video–language pretraining: VideoBERT, VideoCLIP, MERLOT

  • VideoBERT: turn video features into visual words, then train BERT on video words + speech text
  • VideoCLIP: contrastive learning with overlapping clips and harder negatives
  • MERLOT: frames + transcripts; learn what happens and in what order
  • Lessons kept by modern Video-LLMs: speech as free labels, time alignment
Ready

Connectors: projector, Q-Former, Perceiver resampler, pixel shuffle

  • Linear or MLP projector (LLaVA): simple and strong
  • Q-Former (BLIP-2): learned queries pull a fixed number of tokens from the image
  • Perceiver resampler (Flamingo): a fixed set of latents via cross-attention
  • Pixel shuffle and patch merging: fewer tokens by folding neighbors into channels
